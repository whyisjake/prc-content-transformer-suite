Americans are navigating a rapidly changing information landscape. Eight-in-ten U.S. adults say they get news from digital devices at least sometimes, while just one-in-five say they prefer print sources. For more on these trends, see the Pew Research Center journalism project.

Key Findings on Media Consumption

The shift toward digital news consumption has accelerated significantly over the past decade. Younger adults are far more likely than older adults to rely on social media for news.

Social Media as a News Source

Among adults under 30, social media has surpassed television as the most common pathway to news. This trend holds across partisan groups, though the preferred platforms differ.

Platform Preferences by Age

Facebook remains dominant among adults 50 and older, while YouTube and Instagram lead among younger cohorts. TikTok's share of news consumption has grown threefold since 2020.

Trust Levels Vary by Platform

Trust in news consumed via social media is significantly lower than trust in direct news outlet sources, regardless of age group or political affiliation.

Methodology Note

This survey was conducted online among a nationally representative sample of 5,140 U.S. adults. The margin of error is plus or minus 1.7 percentage points.

About This Report

This report is based on data from a survey of 5,140 U.S. adults conducted Jan. 13–19, 2025. Everyone who took part is a member of the Center's American Trends Panel, an online survey panel that is recruited through national, random sampling of residential addresses. Address-based sampling ensures nearly all U.S. adults have a chance of selection.

The data above illustrates the divergence between platform reach and platform trust. Platforms that reach the largest audiences are not necessarily those that audiences trust most when it comes to news content.
